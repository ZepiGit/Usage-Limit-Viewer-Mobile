# Architecture

Provider adapters turn responses into shared account, quota and balance models. Screens and widgets use those models rather than interpreting provider JSON. Android uses Kotlin, Compose, Room and Glance; iOS uses SwiftUI, WidgetKit and the shared `UsageLimitsKit` package.

## Redesign scope

This describes the integrated Android and iOS software as shipped in 0.5.1 on `main`. Both native apps have Overview, Resets and Settings. List shows every usage window; Dials uses the same headline and automatic account order without List filters.

[ADR 0001](adr/0001-one-presentation-per-usage-window.md) and [ADR 0002](adr/0002-one-account-headline-per-platform.md) define shared window presentation and account headlines. [ADR 0004](adr/0004-automatic-overview-order-and-stored-order.md) separates automatic Overview/All accounts ranking from stored pin and other selection order. [ADR 0003](adr/0003-status-icons-and-lock-screen-substitute.md#consequences) retains SystemUI's ordering limits, and [ADR 0005](adr/0005-app-and-widget-appearance.md) separates app appearance from widget styling. Retained branch histories include obsolete prototypes for provenance, not as enabled experiments.

## Code layout

Android has one `:app` Gradle module. `core/` contains models, networking, authentication, storage, settings and sync. `providers/` owns each service's endpoints and parsers. `feature/` contains screens and ViewModels, `ui/` shared presentation, and `widget/` home-screen rendering. `AppContainer` wires dependencies through constructors.

On iOS, `UsageLimitsKit` owns provider clients, authentication, models and sync. The app handles browser presentation and background work; the widget extension reads the published App Group snapshot. The platforms share behavior, not a database or credentials.

## Quota model

`ProviderAccount` identifies an account by provider and external account ID. Its `credentialReference` names a separate credential-store entry. Nonsecret routing details, such as region or project ID, live in account attributes.

`UsageWindow` stores percent consumed and derives percent remaining. Adapters convert remaining fractions and amount/capacity pairs at this boundary. Missing or invalid measurements stay unknown. Window categories follow declared durations; unfamiliar durations retain their labels and reset times. Global constraints, model or feature pools, and unknown scopes remain distinct.

Kimi and Z.ai window IDs use stable provider fields rather than response positions. Both parsers read the same 16 cases in [`stable-provider-window-ids.json`](../shared/test-vectors/stable-provider-window-ids.json). Indistinguishable duplicate windows use occurrence suffixes; reordered identical entries cannot establish an individual identity. Cached old IDs remain readable until a fresh successful response replaces the windows. Account pins and widget account selections do not change. On iOS, alert-ledger entries for colliding category/label groups include the window ID, so the transition can re-arm a warning once; it does not guarantee zero repeated alerts.

`UsageSnapshot` records measurement time, attempt time, status, windows and reset credits. Failed refreshes retain the previous readings and their original age. Partial results keep measurements alongside a separate warning. The Kit keeps the raw scope-aware `quotaSeverity` separate from error validity, so a failed retained model-specific restriction can still mean Limited rather than global exhaustion. This does not make the retained reading current.

Severity follows remaining quota: above 50% is healthy, above 20% moderate, above zero low, and zero exhausted. Global limits govern availability; an exhausted model pool can restrict that model while other quota remains. Summaries prefer global constraints rather than averaging independent pools. Credit balances have a separate decimal-money model described in [Credits](credits.md).

The account headline is the one window that speaks for an account at a given moment, and it is defined once per platform in the model layer: `UsageSnapshot.headlineAt` in `core/model/Usage.kt` and `headline(at:)` in the Kit's `Model/Headline.swift`, which the widget export's `GlanceAccount.representative(at:)` also uses. Reset-due windows drop out unless none would remain, global windows outrank scoped ones, and an exhausted window, then the lowest remaining percentage, wins; ties go to the first window in provider order. Behaviour both platforms must share is pinned by hand-written JSON vectors in `shared/test-vectors/`, which `HeadlineVectorsTest` and `HeadlineVectorsTests` read from the repository rather than from copies.

Without a measurement time, Android app rows and widget rows cannot establish that a reset followed the reading, so they do not mark it reset due; iOS widget rows conservatively treat a passed reset as due. This does not change account-headline selection: both platforms treat a passed reset with no fetch time as due there, as the shared headline vectors require. The former cross-account lead strip is no longer an Android surface. Overview and All accounts use [ADR 0004's automatic order](adr/0004-automatic-overview-order-and-stored-order.md); other widget selections keep their own rules.

## Presentation and localization

Every screen and widget presents a usage window through one `WindowPresentation`, never through its own copy of the rules. It carries the figure token, the side the figure counts (left or used), the fill fraction and an exhausted flag, and holds no strings or colors. `core/model/WindowPresentation.kt` on Android and `Model/WindowPresentation.swift` in the Kit have the same names and rules, and `WindowPresentationTest` and `WindowPresentationTests` read the presentation and fill-state tables from [`shared/test-vectors/window-presentation.json`](../shared/test-vectors/window-presentation.json). Before rounding, the numeric Left and Used values are complements derived from remaining quota. Displayed tokens can round independently, and exhaustion takes precedence in Used mode. `Severity` stays keyed on remaining in both modes.

The reading's validity and the fill state decide how a bar is painted. `ReadingValidity` (current, stale, reset due, error) outranks severity: a stale or reset-due reading keeps its figure under a grey fill, and an error-validity presentation draws no fill. After a failed sync, Android's Overview List gives the account's retained windows stale validity, so each keeps its last figure over a grey bar, while Dials gives the same stored reading error validity and draws its ring without fill. Neither presents that reading as fresh: both keep the last figure and the failure message, and this existing List/Dials difference is not the widget rule in [Widgets](widgets.md). Only a current reading lets severity decide, where an exhausted window draws no fill in Left but is the full bar in Used. `FillState` (data, stale, no fill) is the result; each surface maps it to its own palette, and Glance widgets restate the app's because they cannot read its theme. Two ring surfaces choose their ink independently of fill state: Android widget ring arcs use the widget data color, or grey for stale, failed, reauthentication-needed and reset-due readings, and the iOS circular Lock Screen arc always uses the primary color. Both still take the fraction from the presentation.

Words and the percent sign stay with the surface. Pure code that has no resources receives them through adapters, with English as the default so tests need no resources. Android passes `CountdownTexts` and `NotificationTexts`, implemented in `ui/text` by `ResourceCountdownTexts` and `ResourceNotificationTexts`. The Kit declares protocols of the same names, `CountdownTexts` and `NotificationTexts`, and the app target implements them with `String(localized:)` in `LocalizedTexts.swift`. The Kit therefore performs no resource lookups and stays compatible with plain Foundation on Linux. Its English text implementations remain as defaults; app and widget entry points inject localized implementations. A few English functions remain only as references that tests hold equal to the resources.

Strings live in one catalog per platform: Android's `values` and `values-de`, and iOS's `Localizable.xcstrings`, shared by the app and the widget extension. Both ship English and German. The percent sign comes from a template, so the German app reads “73 %”. Widgets attach it directly on both platforms and in every language (“73%”); see [Widgets](widgets.md). Provider-reported window labels and model names are data and are not translated. `StringResourcesCompletenessTest` checks that every Android string exists in both languages, and `ios/Tools/check-xcstrings.py` checks the iOS catalog and the literals in the app and widget sources.

Both apps choose text and figure faces independently in Settings → Appearance, with four palettes and Pure black as the default. Stand-alone percentages, stamps and amounts use figure roles; reset sentences and other prose use text roles. The six [canonical bundled families and full notices](../third_party/fonts/README.md) remain available offline. Android's system text asks `sans-serif` for weights 450 and 560 on the `wght` axis without synthesis; unsupported device fonts ignore that axis. iOS lists Apple's System text first and uses native CoreText variations for bundled variable fonts.

Android reads the saved theme and fonts before its app root draws. On iOS, `settingsLoaded` gates edits and saves: `load()` reads and applies persisted preferences before `prepareForUse()` can suspend. Later cache loads cannot overwrite those installed preferences. `RootView` shows only `LaunchNeutral` until they are ready, then applies `appAppearance` through the local `usageAppearance` environment. Its default `.widget` context keeps Warm dark, system text and native system-monospaced figures. Canonical fonts and notices belong only to the app target; neither the widget extension nor its snapshot receives app font preferences or resources. `WidgetInk` and widget-styled previews retain that boundary ([ADR 0005](adr/0005-app-and-widget-appearance.md)). These source contracts do not establish physical first-frame or font-rendering results.

Absolute reset stamps use local calendar dates: time today, weekday and time one through six dates ahead, otherwise date and time. Both suites read [`reset-stamp-tier.json`](../shared/test-vectors/reset-stamp-tier.json), including daylight-saving boundaries; elapsed 24-hour periods do not select the tier.

The app's display mode is the only choice for every screen and usage widget. Android widgets and their configuration preview read the app setting; iOS widgets read it from the published snapshot. Widget configuration controls selection and appearance, never the display mode.

## Motion

App-defined motion takes its timing from named specs: `stateChange` (120 ms), `contentSwap` (180 ms), `move` (240 ms), and the stages of a fade-through. Android defines them in `ui/theme/Motion.kt`. iOS keeps the timings in the Kit's `Presentation/Motion.swift` and applies them in `Theme/UsageMotion.swift`.

[`shared/test-vectors/motion.json`](../shared/test-vectors/motion.json) holds each motion's duration and delay, and `MotionVectorsTest` and `MotionTests` read it. That is what the two platforms are held to: the same motion lasts and waits the same time. It is not frame-by-frame sameness. Android fades linearly and eases position changes; iOS uses SwiftUI's ease-in-out. Android interruption tests inspect rendered frames; the timing vectors say nothing about interruption or frame pacing. Three Android motions have no iOS counterpart, because the system plays that part there: the launch pair and the back gesture's fade.

Both apps configure a fade-through for list changes and for a figure and its side word together ("60% left"). Bars move to new lengths for the same window. A different source window or account starts a new presentation instead of interpolating from the previous reading. First load and deep-link filter resets do not request list motion.

Sign-in steps use one displayed card: 90 ms exit, content replacement at zero opacity, then 150 ms entry. An interruption continues from the current presentation value rather than restarting at full opacity. Outgoing content accepts neither input nor accessibility actions. Reduced motion replaces the card directly. Native rendering and device pacing remain acceptance checks, not consequences of matching timing constants.

App-defined motion uses the platform preference: Android's animator duration scale and iOS Reduce Motion. On iOS, Debug builds can override that preference with `-reduce-motion`. `MotionUITests` checks the real modifiers' decisions through `-motion-log` and the sign-in fixture's system accessibility tree, not the resulting transactions or rendered frames. A wait still needs a visible indicator: iOS uses a still mark under Reduce Motion and retains the credit dialog's progress representation.

System motion stays the system's: tab switches, pushes, sheets, alerts and pull to refresh on iOS; dialogs, snackbars and switches on Android. On Android, tabs fade through in place. Push and pop also shift along the reading axis. The bottom bar and side rail stop taking taps, focus or accessibility actions as soon as they leave, and become interactive when the entrance finishes. A width change swaps the bar and rail without animating. A back gesture previews the underlying route with motion on. With motion off it does not preview; only a completed gesture pops the route. Reduced navigation does not animate the bar or screen, but their changes need not appear on the same frame: the chrome follows the route immediately, while Navigation Compose can display the previous screen briefly.

Android's app-owned launch overlay uses the selected icon and palette; reduced motion skips it. The first app-controlled frame waits for the saved theme and fonts. On API 33+, the activity registers the saved splash theme for the next launch. API 26–32 retains the manifest's bare Pure black system-owned starting ground, also the accepted first post-upgrade fallback before a saved splash theme is registered. System bars follow the applied palette. The widget configuration activity keeps its separate launch scene and no app overlay. These code paths do not establish cold-frame behavior on a phone; the [device checklist](release.md#device-checklist) retains pre-33, alias, upgrade and navigation-bar checks.

Compose can use its fallback spring when the app's route tweens are interrupted. Material's ripple and selection indicator, and iOS widget animations keep their own timing.

Android's Claude Code figure (shown for its Color and Monochrome choices) is the app's only decorative loop. Its timings, keyframes and still poses come from the design's figure appendix, not from the vocabulary above, but it stops for the same reasons. On Android it moves only while the animator duration scale is above 0, "Animate Claude Code icon" is on, its screen is RESUMED and part of it lies inside its scrolling parent's visible area and the window. Otherwise it draws its mood's still pose. Each figure has one time source, read only while drawing and advanced at most 30 times a second. Under its controlled test clock, `ClaudeCodeFigureCostTest` observes zero recompositions anywhere in the window while each tested screen is shown (Overview List, Overview Dials and Settings). Its layout counter observes zero measure or placement passes at the outer `ProviderLogo` node, not inside the figure. The mood is the account headline's raw percent left (ADR 0002), held to `shared/test-vectors/claude-code-mood.json`. iOS uses the same mood and geometry with its native Canvas/TimelineView renderer, visibility, active-scene and Reduce Motion gates. These controlled Android checks do not measure device frame pacing or battery use, or establish native iOS rendering success.

`claudeCodeStillBitmap` exports the same art without reading app appearance. Placed Usage Bars, Account Rings and Mini Rings call it through the existing `widgetProviderImage` path for `claudecode-color` and `claudecode`. They pass the already projected headline's raw percentage left and exhaustion flag through the same steady-mood helper. Color keeps the figure's artwork; monochrome uses widget text ink, the sleeping glyph uses secondary ink, and the eyes remain transparent. Configuration and other artwork stay static; Summary, Credits and status-icon glyphs are unchanged. No widget animation or Hooray state is involved. The iOS `WidgetProviderMark` uses the existing `ClaudeCodeStill`/Canvas renderer and entry-date headline. Color retains its artwork; monochrome uses `WidgetInk`, with transparent eyes. Configured Bars has the provider slot; static Summary remains logo-free. Non-pinned rings omit the visible side caption but keep the full spoken presentation. Pinned views retain their layout contract.

The quota bar's fill and segment pill read animation values during drawing. The bottom bar reads opacity and position in its layer without changing the slot height on each tick. Those reads do not trigger recomposition; `BarMotionTest` also counts zero measure passes for a fill. The side rail changes layout width, and a figure's changing width can remeasure its row and neighbouring content.

## Storage and sync

Android encrypts credentials with a Keystore key and stores account metadata, readings and widget configuration in Room. iOS uses Keychain credentials and local account files. Widget inputs contain presentation data rather than tokens; see [Security](security.md). Time-horizon summary rows use `summaryLineShort` and `summaryLineLong`, distinct from the single account headline. The iOS snapshot retains its legacy `headlineShort` and `headlineLong` encoded keys for compatibility. `GlanceRow` construction and decoding treat nonfinite or out-of-range percentages as unknown, matching `UsageWindow`.

Android stores one nullable `pendingResetConfirmationAt` on the account row (Room 14→15). `saveSnapshot` uses `confirmedResetWindowIds` to record Claude's dated-cycle evidence in the snapshot transaction; retained/unknown windows, failed reads and first snapshots do not confirm. An existing mark coalesces further confirmations and survives failures and reconnect. `claimPendingResetConfirmations` clears it transactionally, ordered against scheduled commits and removal. A process `ON_START` and that interval's first main-activity `onStart` form one opening in either callback order; a non-main start spends the transition without claiming or requesting an on-open sync. The claim completes before the one on-open sync is enqueued, so its new confirmation waits for the following opening. Activity recreation and resume alone do not trigger reads.

At the first ready layout, each claimed account with a visible real Claude Code slot starts one four-second presentation shared across its slots on the Compose frame clock, independent of animator duration scale. The renderer keeps its existing motion gates and drawing clock; motion off shows the Hooray still for the same duration. Resets and sample previews do not start it. The loaded, resumed navigation content signals its actual first placement, and each real figure synchronously enrolls its visible eligibility in its existing placement callback. Playback samples enrollment once on the next frame timestamp; a count of transition frames is not a layout barrier. Controlled real-navigation tests establish cold-entry and held-first-placement behavior, not the frequency of that delay on phones. Unseen accounts and plain artwork are discarded after that ready layout; leaving the main activity stops playback without replay. Widgets and notification state carry no pending mark.

iOS stores the optional pending mark in the same atomic account-register JSON replacement as its snapshot. The sync writer checks the account instance, so an old in-flight read cannot write into a removed-and-readded account. `AppOpeningCoordinator` loads the cache, claims pending marks, then requests on-open sync. Initial active and background-to-active count; an inactive-only bounce does not. Resolved ready-content and visible-slot anchors start one account-shared four-second presentation, independently of animation being enabled. Interruption or no eligible placement consumes the opportunity without later replay. Neither App Group exports nor preferences contain the mark. Both suites consume all 24 cases in [`reset-confirmation.json`](../shared/test-vectors/reset-confirmation.json) through their production commit/reopen/claim paths; the [release checkpoint](release.md#release-050-checkpoint) separates the parent's portable Kit results from pending review corrections, native execution and final-candidate CI.

Android's account/snapshot DAO read boundary retains the last known account projection when storage becomes unreadable. Its retry resubscribes to that boundary; credits, settings and downstream failures are not relabeled as account-storage errors. Overview and details show a retry card rather than false empty-account wording. A cold account link can show that storage card and recover in place. Reconnect opens the existing Add flow with the provider preselected; it does not introduce another sign-in or identity rule.

Sync isolates failures per account. Token renewal is serialized per credential reference, re-reading credentials inside the lock to protect rotating refresh tokens. Removal and credential replacement are checked before saving results. Cancellation does not become an account error.

Foreground actions use the sync engine directly. Settings offers a background interval of 5, 15, 30, 60 or 180 minutes; the default is 30. Android background work uses WorkManager with a connected-network constraint. At 15 minutes or more it is periodic work enqueued with the `UPDATE` policy. WorkManager's periodic work cannot run more often than every 15 minutes, so a shorter interval uses unique one-time work with an initial delay of the interval, and each run schedules the next. Changing the interval replaces the pending work of the other mode. Doze and battery saver can defer either kind, so the app does not promise an exact cadence. Repeated queued refresh requests collapse into one pass. On iOS, `RefreshCoalescer` runs the app's refreshes one at a time: requests made while a refresh runs share one follow-up refresh, so an account signed in mid-refresh still gets its first reading, widget data and alert evaluation. iOS background refresh follows system scheduling: the interval only sets `BGAppRefreshTaskRequest.earliestBeginDate`, and the system decides how often the app actually runs.

A reading turns stale after twice the interval, but never sooner than 45 minutes, so with a 5-minute interval the threshold stays at 45 minutes.

If a usage or balance read still returns HTTP 429 after its retry budget, both platforms keep usable results and pause automatic reads for the account for at least 15 minutes, honoring a longer `Retry-After` up to six hours. The account shows a pause notice even when its reading is usable. This includes Antigravity's quota hosts and xAI's billing views. Codex's separate reset-credit read is optional: it is tried once per pass, and a 429 falls back to the credits embedded in the usage response without pausing usage refresh. A manual refresh bypasses the pause, renews it if a required read is still throttled, and clears it on an unthrottled success. Skipped reads leave the cached reading and its age unchanged. A stored deadline more than six hours ahead after a clock change is treated as expired.

Android publishes out-of-app state through `AppContainer.publication`. Its three events keep callers independent of the surfaces: `cacheMoved()` repaints widgets, schedules the next presentation boundary and evaluates alerts; `presentationChanged()` repaints and reschedules without evaluating alerts; `notificationsAllowedAgain()` follows the main app's centralized on-resume observation of a persisted false-to-true notification/category eligibility change. It publishes only status icons, posts every pinned account and evaluates no alerts. An ordinary allowed return does not restore a dismissed icon. The OS eligibility read and persisted last observation are not atomic. Initial account and credit reads use the cache event too. A running Credits widget also consumes presentation repaints, recomputing reading age, severity and order without a provider sync. Settings, ordering, removal, widget configuration and elapsed presentation boundaries use the presentation event, so a cosmetic change cannot consume an alert. Every event reads the cache without another provider request. Repaint, scheduling and alert publication fail independently and are logged: a publication failure cannot turn an already-saved user change into an error, while cancellation still propagates. On iOS, account sign-in requests `UsageStore.refresh()`, which normally calls `UsageLimitsContainer.refresh()` to publish widget data, then claims and posts notifications. Presentation updates also handle stale data and elapsed resets without inventing a new measurement. Both platforms honor reduced-motion settings. [Widgets](widgets.md) describes selection and refresh behavior.

Status icons for pinned accounts ([ADR 0003](adr/0003-status-icons-and-lock-screen-substitute.md)) are one more best-effort step of the same module on Android. The cache and presentation events run it after the repaint and the boundary schedule, and a sync runs it before alerts; `notificationsAllowedAgain()` runs it alone. `StatusIconPublisher` in `statusicon/` is told which event it serves: after a sync and on `notificationsAllowedAgain()` it posts every pinned account, so a swiped-away icon is restored; a presentation event updates the groups still showing and posts accounts pinned since the previous publication ([details and limits](status-icons.md#swiping-an-icon-away)). Switching the feature and changing a pin go through `StatusIconChanges`, which stores one change and publishes it as a presentation event before it stores the next.

What a status icon shows is decided in the model layer, not by the renderer or the widget: `statusIcon` in `core/model/StatusIcon.kt` and `StatusIconContent` in the Kit, from the headline's `WindowPresentation`, its reading validity and whether the account needs signing in again. The pinned selection is `pinnedAccountIds` and `PinnedAccounts.select`. iOS stores pins as `ProviderAccount.pinKey` (`pinnedAccountKeys`) and writes them into the snapshot as resolved account ids (`PinnedAccounts.resolve`). Both rules are checked against `shared/test-vectors/status-icon.json` and `pinned-accounts.json`. Both platforms draw the provider glyphs from `shared/glyphs/status-icon-glyphs.json`, checked by `tools/check_glyph_file.py` (see [Provider artwork](providers/assets.md#glyph-path-grammar)).

The iOS snapshot also carries an optional safe `accountLabel`. Pinned speech uses it to distinguish same-provider accounts, with masked-email or stable numbering fallbacks for older snapshots and collisions. All three forms use that identity without changing their pin order or layout.

On iOS the app writes the pinned-accounts switch and the pinned account IDs into the App Group snapshot, not finished icons. The Pinned accounts widget's timeline provider derives each entry at that entry's date (`PinnedAccountsCapture` in the Kit): the headline, its reading validity and the status icon content. Entries are added at the snapshot's presentation boundaries, so a reading that turns stale or a reset that passes appears without the app running, as far as WidgetKit's reload budget allows.

The App Group snapshot keeps its dates exact. A whole-second date is written as an ISO 8601 string, a date with a fraction as seconds since the Foundation reference date, and the reader accepts both, so snapshots from earlier builds still decode. The fraction matters because a reset inside a second can change an account's headline, and the app and a widget entry must rank at the same instant. An earlier build cannot read a snapshot that holds a fractional date, which is nearly every new one: after a downgrade its widgets show "Can't read usage" until that build's app writes the file again.

On Android 13+, first-launch notification permission handling is recorded in the settings DataStore, including existing grants; earlier Android versions need no runtime request. Settings shows current authorization without disabling alert preferences and offers a dialog retry or system settings. Android cannot distinguish dismissal from permanent denial, so both recover through system settings. iOS uses `UNUserNotificationCenter` authorization.

## Widget behaviour reference

Detailed, developer-level widget rules. The user-facing catalog is in [Widgets](widgets.md).

### Android widgets

Open your launcher's widget picker, choose Usage Limits and place a widget. Resize it using the launcher's handles. The four usage styles are:

| Style | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Usage Summary | One leading account's limits and next reset, with selection-wide status labeled separately |
| Usage Bars | Scrollable account cards with limit rows and reset times |
| Account Rings | Scrollable rings with provider icons, account labels and resets |
| Mini Rings | A compact grid of rings and icons; capacity depends on placement size |

At placement, choose closest resets, most critical, all accounts, one account, one provider, or a custom account list and order. The first three include every account with different ordering: all accounts uses Overview's order (sign-in expired first, then the least room left in each account's headline; [ADR 0004](adr/0004-automatic-overview-order-and-stored-order.md)), while one provider keeps the stored account order and a custom list keeps its own. Closest resets is the default. Use the launcher's reconfigure control to change an existing widget. Saving applies the selection; canceling keeps its current configuration.

Each usage widget has its own background color, opacity and text tone. Automatic text tone follows the selected panel color, including at zero opacity; it does not sample wallpaper. Choose light or dark text explicitly when needed on a transparent panel. Provider artwork follows the app's saved icon choices. Android app palettes and text/figure fonts do not restyle placed widgets or their configuration editor; the editor keeps its Warm dark scene and widget-styled preview ([ADR 0005](adr/0005-app-and-widget-appearance.md)).

Account Rings and Mini Rings omit the visible Left/Used caption, while their accessibility descriptions retain the side, headline window, reset and applicable reading state. Summary labels and Bars figures still name the side. A widget tap naming an account opens its detail through Overview; a whole-widget tap opens Overview.

On Android, placed Usage Bars, Account Rings and Mini Rings show a steady mood still when **Claude Code · Color** or **Claude Code · Monochrome** is selected. Each account uses its own headline's raw percentage left; provider-declared exhaustion shows Asleep even with a positive or missing figure. Used mode, reading age and the app's animation switch do not change the mood rule or create a reset celebration. Existing reading publications and scheduled presentation repaints rebuild the headline and still. Color retains the figure's artwork; the monochrome body uses the widget's primary text ink, the sleeping glyph uses secondary ink, and the eyes are transparent cut-outs.

Placed widgets never animate or play Hooray. Configuration keeps fixed catalog artwork; other icon choices remain static. Usage Summary and Credits have no provider logo.

The configuration preview and every usage widget follow the app's display mode. See [Percentages: left or used](widgets.md#percentages-left-or-used).

All accounts ordering is not pin ordering. Android status icons retain the stored account order, independent of automatic Overview ranks; Custom keeps its saved ID order. Status icons are off by default and require notifications and their category to be allowed. A swipe normally hides an icon until the next sync; display or appearance changes do not restore it. The main app's on-resume observer can restore it after a persisted blocked-to-allowed change, without a sync. An ordinary return while already allowed does not restore it; the OS read and saved last observation are not atomic. These permission, dismissal and best-effort SystemUI rules remain in [Status icons](status-icons.md) and the [ADR 0003 clarification](adr/0003-status-icons-and-lock-screen-substitute.md#consequences).

### iOS widgets

The native WidgetKit behaviour below ships in 0.5.1.

Add Usage Limits from the Home Screen widget gallery. On iOS 17 and later, configurable Usage Bars, Account Rings and Mini Rings support account selection, provider selection and saved custom layouts. Edit a custom layout's accounts and order in app Settings; widgets using that layout share those choices. Content, transparency and text-tone choices belong to each widget. App palettes and text/figure fonts do not restyle widgets or their in-app configuration previews: those retain the widget palette, `WidgetInk` and system fonts. The bundled app fonts do not enter the extension.

Configured Usage Bars includes a provider mark, including its single-account small family; static summary tiles remain logo-free. Account Rings, Mini Rings and non-pinned Usage Ring forms omit the visible Left/Used caption and its reserved height. Their spoken descriptions still name the account identity, full figure and side, headline window, reset and reading state.

With Claude Code Color or Monochrome selected, configurable Bars and rings show the entry-date headline's steady mood through the same still renderer as the app. Color retains its artwork, monochrome uses widget ink, and the eyes remain transparent. They never animate or play Hooray. Configuration previews keep static catalog artwork.

The configured **All accounts** choice ranks accounts like Overview: expired sign-ins first, then lowest headline percentage left, with ties by account-added time and ID. The widget evaluates the headline at each timeline entry. Provider keeps register order; Closest Resets and Most Critical keep it on ties. Older widget snapshots keep register order for All accounts until the app publishes again.

On iOS 16, Usage Limits tiles, Usage Limits (Clear), and Usage Ring use the Most Critical ranking. Standard tiles have small, medium, large and rectangular Lock Screen forms; the ring has small and circular Lock Screen forms. These widgets use fixed layouts rather than scrolling lists. They are static widgets on every iOS version, so they always follow the app's percentage setting.

The Pinned accounts widget is the iOS form of the [status icons](status-icons.md). It is off by default. Turn on pinned accounts in app Settings, then pin up to three usage accounts; credit accounts cannot be pinned. Add Pinned accounts to the Lock Screen on iOS 16 or later, in one of three forms. All three follow the app's display mode and keep the account register order, independently of Overview.

- **Rectangular:** one cell per pin, side by side: the provider glyph and the figure without a leading zero. A non-exhausted window at 100 shows only its glyph, larger; an exhausted window shows a crossed circle, a failed sync or expired sign-in "!", and a missing percentage a dash. An underline marks a stale or reset-due figure.
- **Circular:** the first pinned account, drawn like one rectangular cell.
- **Inline:** one line above the clock, showing the first pinned account only, like the circular form: its provider glyph as the line's one image, then its figure with the side, "42% left" / "42% übrig", or a word for a state without a number (Exhausted, Error, Sign-in expired, Unknown). The system draws this form in its own font and drops other styling, so an outdated figure cannot be underlined and says so instead: "42% left · Stale" / "42% übrig · Veraltet". The line names no account; the glyph identifies the provider. More in [Status icons](status-icons.md#ios-lock-screen-forms).

The glyphs are the same silhouettes as Android's status icons, drawn from `shared/glyphs/status-icon-glyphs.json`. Each cell's accessibility label includes provider and plan, a distinct account identity, the full figure and its side, or the state and “Stale” where applicable. It retains “<1%”, “>99%” and 100%; inline uses the same speech for its first account. A safe account label distinguishes same-provider accounts, with masked-email or stable account-number fallbacks for older snapshots and colliding labels. This identity improvement does not change the pinned layout or pin order; the non-pinned caption changes above do not apply to pins.

The widget schedules entries in advance for the moments a reading turns stale or a reset falls due, so these changes do not need the app to be running; when WidgetKit actually displays an entry still depends on the system's update budget. With the feature off or no pins, the rectangular form shows "Pin an account in Settings", the circular form a pin with "Pin in Settings" and the inline form "Pin in Settings". If the widget cannot read the app's data before the first unlock after a restart, it shows "Locked" (the circular form a lock symbol) and asks iOS to reload in 15 minutes; with corrupt snapshot data the rectangular form asks you to open the app to rebuild it, the inline form shows "Can't read usage" and the circular form a warning symbol with that label.

### Percentages: left or used (reference)

On both platforms, **Show percentages as** is in **Settings → Display**, with **Left** (the default) and **Used**. On a German device: **Einstellungen → Anzeige → Prozente anzeigen als**, **Übrig/Verbraucht**. The display mode applies to Overview, account detail and Resets, the widget configuration preview, notification texts and all usage widgets. Credits are money, not percentages, and are unchanged.

- **Switching on Android.** Tap the percentage chip in the Overview header to flip the same setting. Its accessibility label names the other mode. Figures fade through on a switch, except when reduced motion is on.
- **Left or Used.** Android Usage Summary labels and Usage Bars figures name the side. Android Account Rings and Mini Rings leave out the visible caption to make room for the rings; their accessibility descriptions name the side, headline window and reset. Non-pinned iOS rings also omit the visible caption and retain the full side in speech; Pinned accounts keeps its existing visible and spoken treatments. Settings notification titles also follow the mode, for example “Below 20% left” and “Above 80% used”; the thresholds themselves do not change.
- **Fill follows the figure; meaning does not.** The displayed value determines the bar length or ring arc, so Used fills as the window is consumed. Severity, account order, “most critical” ranking and notification thresholds remain decided by what is left. Severity-colored surfaces keep a nearly exhausted usage window red in both modes. Android widget ring arcs use the widget data color, and grey for stale, failed, reauthentication-needed or reset-due readings, rather than a severity color; the iOS circular Lock Screen ring keeps its primary color in every state.
- **Exhausted, unknown and stale.** An exhausted usage window reads 100% used; a current, valid reading fills its bar completely in Used mode. An unknown figure shows a dash and no fill in both modes. Stale and reset-due readings retain their figures and use grey bar fill; failed readings draw no bar fill. Android widget ring arcs turn grey for stale, failed, reauthentication-needed and reset-due readings and otherwise use the widget data color; the iOS circular Lock Screen arc keeps its primary color in every state. Both draw the fraction the presentation gives, even for aged readings. On Account Rings a full allowance (100% left) leaves the ring's centre empty where the ring draws its arc, and reads “100” without the percent sign where it draws none (on iOS, a failed reading or a provider-exhausted window); an exhausted window reads “100” without the percent sign in Used mode; the spoken description still says 100%.
- **The breakdown is always used.** A window's segment breakdown, such as “Opus usage: 20%”, counts what was used in both modes and is labeled that way.
- **German.** On a device set to German the words become “übrig” and “verbraucht”. The app separates the percent sign with a space (“73 %”), but widgets on both platforms attach it directly in every language (“73% übrig”). `shared/test-vectors/widget-percent-format.json` lists the cases; an unknown figure stays a bare dash. Spoken descriptions keep their localized wording. Window labels that a provider reports, and model names, are shown as reported.

Every usage widget follows the app setting, including widgets that previously had their own Left or Used choice. There is no separate widget display-mode setting. Credits show money and are unchanged.

On Android a change in Settings repaints every usage widget. On iOS the app writes its choice into the widget snapshot and asks WidgetKit to reload, so delivery follows the timeline rules below.

### Refresh and reset times

App refreshes, sign-in, removal and reset-credit actions request widget updates. Android's refresh button queues a shared sync with a network constraint. Background sync runs at the interval chosen in Settings: 5, 15, 30 minutes, 1 hour or 3 hours, with 30 minutes as the default. At 15 minutes or more it is periodic work. Android's periodic work cannot run more often than every 15 minutes, so with 5 minutes each sync schedules the next one as one-time work. Doze and battery saver can delay either kind. The widget framework also requests updates around every 30 minutes. Android controls actual background delivery, so the chosen interval is not an exact cadence.

On iOS, refresh links open the app to fetch new readings. The chosen interval is only the earliest time the app asks to refresh in the background; the system decides how often it actually runs, often less often than asked. WidgetKit controls timeline delivery, so saving a configuration or requesting a reload has no fixed display deadline.

Reset times are absolute. An elapsed reset shows “Reset due” until a fetch supplies the next window. Failed refreshes retain the original measurement age; unknown percentages show a neutral state rather than an empty allowance. Partial results carry a separate warning.

Both Credits widgets show balances worst first. Android's smallest tile places the account name at the top and centers the balance and freshness or status below where they fit; at larger text sizes they wrap and may sit off center. The iOS small family centers its title/status mark, 28 pt system-monospaced balance and status; the amount scales when space requires it. Larger families list up to three or six accounts and indicate when more are available.

When the small Credits widget has no room for a full status sentence, it shows a short word: “Not read” / “Ungelesen”, “Read error” / “Lesefehler” or “Update error” / “Updatefehler” (`shared/test-vectors/credit-widget-compact-status.json`). The spoken description keeps the full status, and the cached balance and its age do not change. On Android the tile follows the system font scale, with a nominal 28 sp amount and an 11 sp status, within its 110 × 110 dp minimum; other freshness text keeps its two-line limit and may end in an ellipsis. On iOS the small family's fonts follow Dynamic Type up to its existing caps (20 pt title, 40 pt amount, 14 pt status), so it does not grow to the largest accessibility sizes.

On Android, a running Credits widget consumes scheduled presentation repaints, recalculating age, severity and order without a sync. iOS prepares entries at reading-age boundaries and recalculates order for each entry date. Cached amounts and measurement timestamps stay unchanged. Actual launcher/WidgetKit delivery remains system-controlled. See [Credits](credits.md) for setup.
